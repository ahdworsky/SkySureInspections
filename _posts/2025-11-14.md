# How to Spot Storm Damage from Your Living Room in 5 Minutes

![heroImage](https://cdn.marblism.com/p4eIYdZRdtN.webp)

After a big storm rolls through, you're probably wondering if your roof took a beating. The good news? You don't need to grab a ladder or risk your safety to get a pretty good idea of what's going on up there. In just five minutes from the comfort of your living room, you can spot the telltale signs that storm damage might be lurking above.

Here's your quick damage detective guide that could save you thousands down the road.

## Start With Your Ceiling - Your Roof's Report Card

Look up. Really look up. Your ceiling is basically your roof's report card, and it doesn't lie.

Scan every inch of your ceiling for **water stains, brown spots, or discoloration**. These usually show up as yellowish or brownish rings or patches. They're like breadcrumbs leading back to roof damage above. Don't ignore small spots either - what looks minor today could be a major leak tomorrow.

Pay extra attention to corners where walls meet the ceiling. Water loves to travel along the path of least resistance, and these joints are often the first places you'll see evidence of a problem.

![image_1](https://cdn.marblism.com/9uWExWL8qJT.webp)

Dark spots are especially concerning. If you see any new dark patches on your ceiling that weren't there before the storm, that's water getting through your roof and into your home. Time to take action.

## Trust Your Nose - Smell Test

This might sound weird, but take a deep breath through your nose. Do you smell anything musty, damp, or just... off?

A **musty odor** is often the first sign that water has gotten in somewhere it shouldn't be. Storm damage can create small openings that let moisture seep in, and that moisture creates the perfect breeding ground for mold and mildew.

Walk through a few rooms and give each one the sniff test. If one room smells different from the others, especially if it smells damp, you've found your problem area.

## Floor Detective Work - Look Down

Now look down at your floors. Walk around and feel for anything unusual under your feet.

**Soft spots, warping, or discoloration** in hardwood, laminate, or even carpet can indicate water damage from above. If your floor feels squishy in spots where it didn't before, water is likely making its way down from your roof.

Check your baseboards too. Look for swelling, peeling paint, or signs of water damage along the bottom of your walls. Baseboards are like canaries in the coal mine for water damage - they'll show problems before you see them anywhere else.

## Window and Door Functionality Check

Here's something most people miss: storm damage can affect how your windows and doors work.

Try opening and closing every window and door in your living area. Do any of them stick, jam, or feel different than they did before the storm? **New alignment issues** often indicate that your house has shifted slightly due to wind damage or water infiltration.

Run your hand around window and door frames. Feel for new air leaks or drafts that weren't there before. Gaps in sealing can indicate that storm winds moved things around more than they should have.

![image_2](https://cdn.marblism.com/MBmucWwfRw0.webp)

## Mold Patrol - The Unwanted Guest

Nobody wants to find mold, but it's better to catch it early. Look for any **green, gray, or black spots** on your walls or ceiling. Mold can start growing within 24-48 hours of water exposure, so after a storm, it can appear quickly.

Check corners, areas around windows, and anywhere you might have seen water stains. Mold loves damp, dark places, and storm damage creates the perfect environment for it to thrive.

## Crack Investigation

Take a closer look at your walls and ceilings for **hairline cracks** that might be new. Pay special attention to areas around windows and doors, where houses tend to flex most during storms.

Also look for separation at joints - where walls meet ceilings or where walls meet floors. These separations can indicate that your house experienced more movement during the storm than it should have, possibly due to foundation settling or structural stress from wind.

## What to Do When You Find Something

Found some warning signs during your 5-minute inspection? Don't panic, but don't ignore them either.

**Document everything** with photos. Take pictures of any stains, cracks, or damage you've spotted. This documentation will be valuable for insurance claims and when talking to contractors.

But here's where things get tricky - and dangerous. Your next instinct might be to grab a ladder and climb up on your roof to see what's going on. Don't do it.
